# Moxibustion timing, low-temperature burns, and the skin-check clock — research note

**Date:** 2026-08-09 · **Purpose:** the evidence base behind `MoxaClockPlan` (MoxaSession.swift):
the 10-minute default / 15-minute cap, the 2.5-minute first check, the 5-minute check cadence, the
required-checks regime for older adults, the strap-not-tape advice, and the decision to ship the
photo skin check WITHOUT any computer-vision verdict.

**The honest framing, stated once for every consumer of this file:** no published standard
specifies a session cap, a check interval, or a strap-vs-tape rule. The app's numbers are DESIGN
CHOICES — the practitioner's guidance (10-15 min, look ~every 5) held against burn physics and
burn epidemiology, and kept at the conservative end of everything found. Any Sources-screen copy
citing this file must say "set conservatively from published burn research", never "per the
standard".


## 1. Session durations in practice and literature

The 10-15 minute session is supported, and sits at the conservative low end of actual practice. Textbook/secondary sources consistently give 10-15 min per point for stick (suspended) moxibustion, and RCT per-session durations cluster at 15-30 min (box-moxibustion protocols most often ~30 min); a 2025 knee-OA meta-analysis found indirect moxibustion under 30 min/session across 10-20 sessions effective, and a Delphi consensus for self-administered breech moxibustion settled on 30 min once daily. So for AcuGuide: 10-15 min default is defensible and conservative; ~30 min is a reasonable hard cap. NO published guideline states a numeric maximum continuous heat exposure at one site, and NO published skin-check interval (e.g., "every 5 minutes") exists in any RCT protocol, nursing operating procedure, or guideline I could find — the practitioner's 5-minute rule is practice wisdom, not literature. It is, however, physics-consistent: a measured moxa-box study (CV4, Zhongguo Zhenjiu 2012, PMID 22997790) found skin held >44°C for ~7-10 min at 3-4 cm source distance and >49°C for 2 min at 3 cm, while classic Moritz-Henriques burn data put epidermal injury at ~6 h at 44°C, halving per +1°C (≈11 min at 49°C, ≈6 min at 50°C) — so at the hot end of measured box temperatures, injury-relevant exposure develops on a ~5-15 min timescale, which is exactly the interval a 5-min check covers. Burn epidemiology strongly supports the elderly-mandatory design: indirect moxibustion (not direct) causes most reported burns (75/82 in one 8-year burn-unit series), ~46% are self-administered, most are deep second-degree or worse by the time of presentation (low-temperature burns are painless in the moment), and a 35-patient surgical series had mean age 64.5. Elderly dosing in the literature is directional only ("small cones, gentler dose for elderly/weak constitution" in textbooks); no quantitative elderly protocol exists, and trials in 60-80-year-olds did not reduce dose. No published strapped-vs-taped box comparison was found. The escalate-only photo check has no direct literature analogue either — frame all app numbers as conservative design choices grounded in burn physics and burn epidemiology, not as citations to a standard.

### Key facts

- **[established] Textbook/standard per-point duration for stick (suspended/mild) moxibustion is 10-15 minutes, until the skin flushes mildly red without pain**  
  Multiple textbook-derived sources give 10-15 min per point (some 5-10 min until erythema) with the moxa stick held ~2-3 cm from the skin. The Chinese nursing operating procedure (J Integrative Nursing 2023, based on China's 2008 'Technical Operational Specifications for Acupuncture and Moxibustion', GB/T 21709.1-2008) specifies 2-3 cm distance, heating 'until the patient feels warmth but not burning pain, with mild skin flush'. The GB/T 21709.1-2008 national standard itself is paywalled; its numeric durations could not be verified directly. — https://journals.lww.com/jinm/fulltext/2023/05030/operating_procedures_of_moxibustion_technology.11.aspx ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/moxibustion ; https://codeofchina.com/standard/GBT21709.1-2008.html
- **[established] RCT per-session durations cluster at 15-30 minutes; there is no consensus optimum**  
  Examples: IBS trial — 30 min over bilateral ST25+ST36, 3x/week for 6 weeks; knee-OA trial — 20 min/session, 3x/week; diabetic peripheral neuropathy trial explicitly compares 15-min vs 30-min arms (twice weekly, 4 weeks) and states existing studies range 'from a few minutes to as long as 30 min' with 'little consensus on the optimal timeframe'. A 2020 review found 28.9% of moxibustion systematic reviews did not even report session number/duration. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12428027/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5075981/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7526112/
- **[established] Box (moxa-box) moxibustion protocols most commonly run ~30 minutes per session**  
  The community self-administered heat-sensitive moxibustion trial for hypertension specified 'one moxa roll (approximately 30 minutes) per session, 1 session/day'; the IBS box protocol used 30 min. I found no box-specific trial using less than 15 min. For a home app, 10-15 min is therefore at the conservative low end of the practiced range, not the middle. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7505380/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5075981/
- **[reported] A 2025 knee-OA meta-analysis found indirect moxibustion under 30 min/session, 10-20 total sessions, the effective dose pattern**  
  Reported in the abstract/summary of 'Efficacy and safety of moxibustion for knee osteoarthritis: A systematic review and meta-analysis' (Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice, 2025): indirect moxibustion should last less than 30 minutes per session with 10-20 sessions total. Full text is paywalled; number seen in abstract-level material only. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1744388125000441
- **[established] The only formal consensus on self-administered moxibustion dosing (breech presentation, Smith 2014 Delphi, cited by Cochrane 2023) is 30 min once daily for at least 10 days**  
  Cochrane review CD003928 (Coyle 2023, 13 trials, 2,181 women) notes a Delphi consensus for self-administration by pregnant women: smokeless moxa sticks, once daily for 30 minutes, ≥10 days, starting 34-35 weeks — while stressing 'there is no accepted protocol'. This is the closest thing to a published home-use dosing consensus in any population. — https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003928.pub4/full
- **[established] Is 10-15 min/session supported? Yes — as a conservative low-end choice, not as the literature's central value**  
  Per-point stick guidance (10-15 min) matches it exactly; per-session trial practice (15-30 min, box usually ~30) sits above it. Nothing in the literature suggests 10-15 min is too long; several sources suggest longer is common. A hard cap around 30 min per session is defensible from the trial corpus and the 2025 KOA dose finding. — Synthesis of the sources above
- **[thin] No published recommendation exists for a maximum continuous heat exposure at one site**  
  Searched PubMed-indexed reviews, the STRICTOM reporting standard, Korean CPG literature, WHO acupuncture safety guidance, and nursing procedures: none states a numeric maximum minutes-at-one-point. The closest published quantitative anchors are the safe-distance study (fact below) and general burn time-temperature thresholds. State this plainly in any 'sources' UI: the app's cap is a design choice, not a cited standard. — Absence across all sources searched; see STRICTOM https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2005290117300158 and WHO-derived guidance https://www.physio-pedia.com/Acupuncture_Contraindications
- **[established] Measured moxa-box skin temperatures: >44°C sustained 7-10 min at 3-4 cm source distance; >49°C for 2 min at 3 cm; 2 cm intolerable — 3-4 cm concluded as the safe distance**  
  Xu PC, Lee TL, Cui SL, Zhongguo Zhenjiu 2012;32(7):611-4 (PMID 22997790, Chinese): moxa box at Guanyuan (CV4) — at 4 cm the skin stayed over 44°C (44.1±1.3 to 46.7±1.5°C) for 7 min; at 3 cm over 44°C (up to 49.3±2.0°C) for ~10 min and over 49°C for 2 min; at 2 cm observation stopped due to burning pain. Conclusion: keep moxa 3-4 cm from skin for both stick and box moxibustion. This is the single best quantitative basis for the app's check cadence. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22997790/ ; https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/pt/wpr-280851
- **[established] Burn-injury physics: epidermal injury threshold ~44°C at the dermo-epidermal junction; ~6 h at 44°C, time-to-injury halves per +1°C over 44-51°C (≈11 min at 49°C, ≈6 min at 50°C); thermal pain onset ~44.4±2.1°C**  
  Moritz & Henriques (1947) time-temperature relationship, restated in 'A review of the evidence for threshold of burn injury' (Martin & Falder, Burns 2017, PMID 28536038) — which also warns these thresholds have been widely misapplied, so present them as order-of-magnitude anchors, not exact limits. Thermal pain threshold 44.4±2.1°C and 'tissue injury if maintained over ~45°C' are reported in the moxibustion TRPV-channel literature. Combined with the moxa-box measurements above, temperatures a box can reach (48-50°C) produce injury-relevant exposure on a ~5-15 minute timescale — a 5-minute skin-check interval is consistent with this physics, but is a derived rationale, not a published recommendation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28536038/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27400483/
- **[thin] No published skin-check interval for moxibustion exists (nothing mandating checks every N minutes)**  
  Searched RCT protocols, the J Integrative Nursing operating procedure, WHO safety guidance, Korean CPGs, and home-use trial instructions: monitoring guidance is qualitative only — maintain warm-not-painful sensation, observe the skin and ask the patient during treatment, never leave burning moxa unattended, stop on adverse signs. The practitioner's 'check every ~5 minutes' is practice wisdom; the app can honestly describe it as a conservative interval consistent with measured box temperatures and burn time-temperature data, and should not cite it to any guideline. — Absence across https://journals.lww.com/jinm/fulltext/2023/05030/operating_procedures_of_moxibustion_technology.11.aspx , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7505380/ , and all trial protocols reviewed
- **[established] Indirect moxibustion — the 'safer' kind — causes the large majority of reported moxibustion burns, and most present as deep burns**  
  Northeast China burn-unit 8-year series (J Burn Care Res 2020): 82 patients, burns from indirect moxibustion in 75/82 vs direct in 7/82; 59/82 deep partial thickness and 7/82 full thickness; most lesions <2 cm diameter; lower extremities most common site (46/82). Korean epidemiology (Burns 2016, PMID 27156790): 59 patients, mean age 49.1; 45.7% self-administered; deep second/third-degree at 44 of 68 sites; non-abdominal sites 9.37x more likely to be deep burns. Eur J Plast Surg 2020 surgical series: 35 patients, mean age 64.5, 71% women, joint pain the most common indication, 65.7% needed local-flap surgery, average 22.6 days from burn to treatment — i.e., these burns are painless enough at the time that people present late. This is the strongest literature support for mandatory periodic checks and an escalate-only posture. — https://academic.oup.com/jbcr/article-abstract/41/1/184/5554816 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27156790/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00238-020-01717-w
- **[established] Low-temperature burns are the characteristic moxibustion injury, and reduced heat sensation (elderly, diabetic neuropathy) is the named risk factor**  
  The 2014 systematic review of moxibustion case reports (Park et al., PMID 24976851) found 43 burn cases among 64 adverse events; most burns 'were in newborns, children, and individuals with sensory disabilities'; it recommends 'controlling for time, distance, dose, and protection' and identifies 3-4 cm as the apparent safe distance, but gives no session-duration limit or check interval. Home-use guidance uniformly tells people with reduced sensation (neuropathy, diabetes) not to self-treat, and warns against falling asleep during treatment. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058265/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24976851/
- **[reported] Elderly-specific dosing in the literature is directional only: 'small moxa cones / gentler dose for elderly, children, and weak constitution' — no quantitative elderly protocol exists**  
  Textbook doctrine (aggregated on ScienceDirect Topics) adjusts cone size and moxa amount by 'pathological condition, general constitution, age, and site': large cones/more moxa for strong constitution and acute conditions; SMALL cones for the elderly, young children, and the weak. No trial or guideline specifies reduced minutes for older adults; notably, a PLOS One RCT protocol in healthy 60-80-year-olds used 60-minute sessions, 5x/week for 12 weeks at 3 cm distance with NO age-specific heat adjustment — so the app's elderly-mandatory checks go beyond, not against, published practice. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/moxibustion ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0301673
- **[established] Skin-surface temperature under moxibustion can reach injury-capable levels: up to ~65°C surface / 45°C subcutaneous reported for indirect moxibustion; burning moxa tip is 548-890°C; heat penetrates 3-4 cm of tissue**  
  MRI/thermocouple study of moxibustion thermal fields (Evid Based Complement Alternat Med 2020, PMC7071799) — the experimenters deliberately kept surface temperature below 60°C to avoid scarring, and cited ~65°C surface / 45°C subcutaneous maxima for indirect moxibustion from prior measurements. Confirms a box that is 'indirect' can still put skin far above the 44-45°C injury threshold if too close or too long. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7071799/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32190089/
- **[reported] Japanese-style direct moxibustion (okyu) is a different dosing universe — rice-grain cones burning seconds each — and its durations do not transfer to box timing**  
  Japanese okyu uses thread-to-rice-grain-size cones (mg quantities) burned in seconds, repeated several times per point; Chinese-style suspended/box moxibustion is the tradition the 10-30 min figures come from. Any citation of 'Japanese practice' for session length would be comparing different techniques. — https://www.cstcm.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Additional-Moxibustion-articles.pdf ; https://www.nuhs.edu/how-chinese-korean-and-japanese-acupuncture-differ/
- **[thin] No published comparison of strapped-on vs taped moxa boxes was found**  
  Nothing in the trial or safety literature compares fixation methods. The only related published detail: the hypertension self-moxibustion protocol wraps the box in a velvet bag as a burn barrier. Treat the strap-over-tape advice as practitioner guidance without a literature citation. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7505380/ (velvet-bag detail); otherwise absence of evidence
- **[established] Reporting standards exist (STRICTOM) but moxibustion trial dose reporting is poor, which is why no canonical duration exists**  
  STRICTOM (extension of CONSORT/STRICTA for moxibustion) requires reporting session frequency and duration; compliance across 34 assessed RCTs averaged 68% (range 33-100%), and 28.9% of systematic reviews omitted session number/duration entirely. Useful context if the app ever needs to explain why it cites a range rather than a single standard number. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2005290117300158 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7526112/

## 2. Low-temperature burn physics and elderly skin

Low-temperature contact burns have a well-established quantitative backbone: Moritz & Henriques (1947) showed no cutaneous burn occurs below 44 C, that ~6 h at 44 C produces irreversible transepidermal necrosis, and that between 44 and ~51 C the time-to-injury roughly HALVES per +1 C (codified in ASTM C1055). Anchored at 44 C = 6 h, the halving rule gives: 45 C ~3 h, 46 C ~90 min, 47 C ~45 min, 48 C ~22 min, 49 C ~11 min, 50 C ~5-6 min, 51 C ~3 min; above ~51 C the curve steepens further (60 C ~5 s, 70 C ~1 s). Japanese clinical/consumer guidance for 低温やけど is more conservative: 44 C = 3-6 h, 46 C = 30-60 min, 50 C = 2-3 min. Pain onset (~43 C) sits only ~1 C below the injury floor, and thermal damage continues AFTER the source is removed (Stoll & Chianta: ~1/3 of total damage post-removal at high heat) — which independently supports AcuGuide's escalate-only photo check. Moxa boxes measurably reach the damage zone: with 3 cm moxa-to-skin spacing, skin hits 45.9-47.8 C within 1 min and stayed >44 C for ~10 min (>49 C for ~2 min) in the classic distance study; at 4 cm it stays ~40-45 C. Burning moxa itself is 548-890 C, so small geometry errors matter. Case series confirm the danger profile: Korean burn-center series (59 patients) — 65% of sites deep-dermal or full-thickness; a 2020 plastic-surgery series of 35 moxibustion burns — mean age 64.5, mean 22.6 days before seeking care, 65.7% needed flap surgery; Beijing low-temperature-burn series — 98.5% third-degree despite tiny size, 71% needing surgery. Elderly physiology stacks every risk: epidermis thins ~6.4%/decade and dermis ~20% by age 60 (shorter heat path), warm-detection thresholds rise distally with age until warm-detection and heat-pain thresholds can become indistinguishable in the lower limb (the subjective alarm fails exactly where moxa is used), skin blood flow and vasodilation decline (less heat carried away), and diabetic neuropathy abolishes the warning entirely. CHECK-INTERVAL VERDICT: a 5-minute check is physics-supported ONLY if the device keeps skin at or below ~48 C — at 47-48 C the necrosis threshold is 22-45 min, so a 5-min cadence interposes 4-9 checks before injury. At 49-50 C (reachable at 3 cm spacing) time-to-injury is ~5-11 min and can be crossed within a single interval — the FIRST check must come earlier (2-3 min, when the box reaches steady state) and the interval alone is insufficient. At >=51 C no check schedule can prevent injury; only distance/design (>=3-4 cm spacing, per the safe-distance study) and instant removability help — which is exactly why the practitioner's strapped-on-not-taped advice is physics-aligned. Things that do NOT exist, plainly: no published elderly-specific time-temperature curve (only a pediatric recommendation to lower protection temps 3-4 C); no study validating any specific skin-check interval for moxibustion (5 min is a defensible derived margin, not a literature standard); and moxibustion case reports almost never document how long the device was left on (the one systematic review says duration was "not systematically documented"). The 10-15 min session cap is conservative versus typical 15-30 min practice and versus the conservative 46 C/30-60 min threshold, and matches the ~7-10 min above 44 C measured in real box sessions.

### Key facts

- **[established] 44 C is the floor for cutaneous burn injury; at 44 C irreversible epidermal injury (transepidermal necrosis) takes ~6 hours**  
  Moritz & Henriques 1947 (Am J Pathol 23:695-720): below 44 C no burn occurs regardless of exposure time; at 44 C, ~6 h of contact produces irreversible damage to the epidermis. Pain perception begins just above 43 C — only ~1 C of subjective warning below the injury threshold. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19970955/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28536038/
- **[established] Between 44 and ~51 C, time-to-injury halves for each +1 C (ASTM C1055); derived table: 45 C ~3 h, 46 C ~90 min, 47 C ~45 min, 48 C ~22 min, 49 C ~11 min, 50 C ~5-6 min, 51 C ~3 min**  
  ASTM C1055 codifies: epidermal damage at ~44 C after 6 h continuous contact, exposure time to skin damage reduced ~50% per 1 C increase up to ~51 C; above that the slope steepens sharply (commonly quoted: ~5 s at 60 C, ~1 s at 70 C). The intermediate values (45-51 C) are geometric interpolation from the 44 C/6 h anchor via the halving rule — mechanically derived, individually less precise than the anchor. Rate of superficial-dermal damage increases logarithmically with linear temperature rise (Martin & Falder 2017). Caveat from Martin & Falder: the M&H data is widely misquoted; the superficial-burn relationship is reliable but deep-burn time-temperature extrapolations have limited clinical validation. — https://www.astm.org/Standards/C1055.htm ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305417917302152 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328631160_Predictor_of_the_depth_of_burn_injuries_A_time-temperature_relationship
- **[reported] Japanese low-temperature-burn (低温やけど) clinical guidance is more conservative: 44 C = 3-6 h, 46 C = 30-60 min, 50 C = 2-3 min to burn**  
  Widely used by Japanese medical associations and the pocket-warmer industry association for consumer heating devices (kairo, hot water bottles, electric blankets); a sensible margin-bearing basis for app thresholds. Reversible epidermal damage occurs sooner than necrosis: reported at ~60 min at 44 C and ~45 min at 46.5 C. — https://www.saiseikai.or.jp/medical/column/low_temperature_burns/ ; https://www.kairo.jp/useful/yakedo/ ; https://nara.med.or.jp/for_residents/5957/
- **[reported] Thermal damage continues after the heat source is removed — up to ~1/3 of total damage accrues post-removal at high heat**  
  Stoll & Chianta showed Moritz & Henriques failed to account for damage occurring after source removal (~one third of total damage at high heat per their estimate), so M&H-based models may underestimate depth. Direct design implication: a photo skin check that can only escalate to STOP (never green-light continued heating) is the correct polarity — skin that already looks red is on a damage trajectory that removal alone does not immediately halt. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7195554/
- **[reported] Moxa boxes reach the damage zone at skin level: 45.9-47.8 C within 1 min at 3 cm spacing; skin >44 C for ~10 min and >49 C for ~2 min during box moxibustion at CV4; ~40-45 C at 4 cm; intolerable at 2 cm**  
  Xu, Lee & Cui, Zhongguo Zhen Jiu 2012;(12):611-614 (n=3 healthy adults, moxa stick and moxa box at ST36/CV4, infrared thermography): at 4 cm distance skin 40.0+/-2.0 to 44.9+/-2.3 C with >44 C lasting ~7 min; at 3 cm, 45.9+/-3.0 to 47.8+/-2.0 C within 1 min, >44 C ~10 min, >49 C ~2 min; at 2 cm observation aborted for burning pain. Authors recommend 3-4 cm as the safe moxa-to-skin distance. Small n — treat as indicative, not definitive. Burning moxa itself measures 548-890 C (Solovchuk 2020), and simulation shows closer spacing or hotter burn pushes skin surface to 60-70 C (PMC9225914), so centimeter-scale geometry errors move skin across the entire 44-51 C danger band. — https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/pt/wpr-280851 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7071799/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9225914/
- **[reported] The therapeutic sweet spot for moxa heat is 46-48 C — inside the burn-capable band, so time limits and checks are load-bearing, not optional**  
  Chinese moxibustion-efficacy literature: stimulation at 46-48 C minimizes both patient discomfort and moxa-heat damage while activating TRPV channels (TRPV1 activates >42 C). At 46-48 C the ASTM-derived time-to-necrosis is 22-90 min: safe for a bounded session, injurious if left unattended — the entire safety model reduces to bounding exposure time. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S025462721630036X ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9225914/
- **[established] Real-world moxibustion burns skew old and deep: mean age 64.5, 65-66% deep-dermal/full-thickness, 65.7% needing flap surgery, mean 22.6 days before seeking care**  
  Two burn-center series. Korea (Burns 2016, 59 patients/68 sites, mean age 49.1, 73% women): superficial second-degree at 21 sites vs deep second/third-degree at 44 sites (65%); non-abdominal sites 9.37x more likely to be deep. Eur J Plast Surg 2020 (35 patients, 2017-2019): mean age 64.46, 71% women, most common motive joint pain, lower extremities 50% of sites, mean wound 7.81 cm2, mean 22.6 days from injury to treatment, local flap needed in 65.7%. The elderly-skewed age, distal-site depth multiplier, and multi-week presentation delay all argue for aggressive in-app stop guidance rather than wait-and-see. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27156790/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00238-020-01717-w
- **[established] Burns are the dominant moxibustion adverse event (43 of 64 cases, 67%), concentrated in people who cannot feel or report heat**  
  Xu et al. 2014 systematic review of case reports (64 AEs, 24 articles, 6 countries): 43 burns; most burns in newborns, children, and individuals with sensory disabilities; ages newborn to 82; risk factors include sensory impairment/paralysis, moxa-to-skin distance under 3-4 cm, very young/old age, diabetes. Application duration in burn cases was NOT systematically documented — the literature cannot tell you how long the devices were left on. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058265/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965229910000762
- **[established] Low-temperature burns from heating devices are small but almost always third-degree: 98.5% third-degree in the Beijing series, 70.9% requiring surgery, 42.2% presenting >3 weeks late**  
  Xie et al., Int Wound J 2023 (Beijing): burn sizes 0.2-5% TBSA (85.9% under 1%), third-degree in 98.54%, surgery in 70.87%, presentation >3 weeks post-injury in 42.23%; commonest devices were heating pads, hot-water bags, electric blankets. Corroborated by an integrative review of body-heating-device burns (10 studies, 1343 patients): low TBSA but partial-to-full thickness, surgery 15-87.4% (hot water bottles) and 91% (wheat bags). Mechanism of deceptive depth: moderate heat penetrates dermis before visible surface change, so wounds look trivial early — another reason the photo check must never reassure. — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/iwj.14085 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32804667/
- **[established] Elderly skin burns faster for four stacked reasons, each quantified: thinner skin (epidermis -6.4%/decade, dermis -20% by 60), degraded warm sensing (warm-detection and heat-pain thresholds converge distally), reduced skin blood flow, and diabetic neuropathy overlap**  
  (1) Structure: epidermal thickness falls ~6.4% per decade, epidermis 5-30% thinner by 60, dermis ~20% thinner by 60, dermal-epidermal junction flattens, epidermal turnover down up to 50% — shorter conduction path to dermis and slower healing. (2) Sensation: QST shows warm-detection thresholds rise with age in a distal-to-proximal pattern (feet worst — exactly where moxa is applied); Guergova & Dufour's review reports warm-detection and heat-pain thresholds get closer with age and 'may be indistinguishable' in the lower limb, i.e., the first thing an elderly user feels may already be injurious heat. (3) Vasculature: aging reduces NO-mediated cutaneous vasodilation and superficial skin blood flow (30% decrease in venular cross-sectional area), so less heat is convected away and the same surface temperature yields a higher dermal temperature. (4) Diabetes: dermal neurovascular dysfunction impairs both C-fiber-mediated vasodilation and warm sensation — burns occur without being felt. Net effect: for older users the pain alarm that normally fires at ~43 C fires late or never, which is the specific failure a mandatory timed visual check compensates for. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568163710000450 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16221286/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4292080/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7297481/ ; https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/24/8/1468/21555/
- **[established] No elderly-specific time-temperature burn curve exists in the literature; the only age-adjusted guidance is pediatric (lower protection temperatures by 3-4 C)**  
  Martin & Falder 2017 state time-temperature relationships have not been established for children's skin and recommend revising maximum temperatures down 3-4 C for pediatric protection; no analogous published curve or offset exists for aged skin. Given thinner elderly skin and converged sensory thresholds, borrowing the pediatric-style downward offset (treating ~44-45 C skin temperature as the elderly action threshold rather than 47-48 C) is a reasonable, clearly-labeled design extrapolation — but it is an extrapolation, not a published standard. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28536038/
- **[reported] Check-interval verdict: 5 minutes is early enough ONLY if skin stays at or below ~48 C; at 49-50 C it is marginal (first check must be at 2-3 min); at >=51 C no interval works and only strap-off-in-seconds design prevents injury**  
  Derived arithmetic from the ASTM halving curve against measured box temperatures — not independently published, and no study validates any specific skin-check interval for moxibustion. At 46 C (time-to-necrosis ~90 min ASTM, 30-60 min conservative Japanese) a 5-min cadence gives 6-18 looks before threshold. At 47-48 C (~22-45 min) it still interposes 4-9 checks — adequate. At 49-50 C (~5.6-11 min, and real boxes at 3 cm measured >49 C) the threshold can be crossed inside one interval, so the first check must land when the box reaches steady state (~1-3 min per the distance study) and escalation must be removal, not observation. At >=51 C (~3 min) checks cannot prevent injury — prevention is 3-4 cm minimum spacing, insulation, and a strapped-on box removable in seconds (taping both delays removal and presses the hot surface into skin). Recommended app logic: cap sessions at 10-15 min (conservative vs typical 15-30 min practice and vs the 46 C/30-60 min conservative threshold; real box sessions measured only ~7-10 min above 44 C); make the check at ~5 min mandatory and non-dismissable for older adults; make the first elderly check earlier (2-3 min); treat any reported/photographed erythema, blistering, numbness, or 'too hot' as immediate stop plus cool-water guidance (damage continues after removal); never let a check extend or restart a session. — Derived from ASTM C1055 / Moritz-Henriques anchors + https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/pt/wpr-280851
- **[reported] Contact-burn context from analogous strapped/worn heat devices: prolonged-contact injuries are a distinct minority mechanism but occur in exactly the sensory-impaired population**  
  UK hot-water-bottle burns-unit series (85 patients, 2004-2013): contact-type (prolonged skin contact, the moxa-box-relevant mechanism) was 18.8% of injuries vs rupture 48.2% and spillage 33%; 9 patients had neurological disorders and 4 had diabetes; mean healing 28.2 days. Winter peak (48 of 85 injuries Dec-Feb). — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3885202/

## 3. Datasets (time-vs-burn, burn images)

(a) No dataset — public or restricted — records moxa/heat-device application time versus skin outcome. The closest thing that exists is the 1947 Moritz & Henriques time–temperature burn tables (human/pig, full text free on PMC): 44°C is the injury threshold and takes ~6 h to damage epidermis, with damage rate rising logarithmically up to 70°C. Moxibustion-specific literature is small-n thermography/temperature-curve studies (skin under a moxa box measured at ~44–49°C at 3 cm spacing; "optimal" stimulation cited as 46–48°C) whose raw data is never published, plus adverse-event case-report reviews (only 64 published moxibustion AE cases ever, 43 of them burns) with no incidence rate and no duration-vs-outcome pairing. Chinese-language literature follows the same pattern: temperature curves in figures, no datasets. (b) Public burn IMAGE datasets are few, tiny, and all show established clinical wounds — not the early erythema an app camera would see: BIP_US (Seville) 94 clinical images; Kaggle/Roboflow sets (~1,300 web-scraped images, provenance/license unusable for a shipped medical-adjacent product); the only skin-tone-diverse clinical set (1,105 burn images, Sweden+South Africa, Fitzpatrick 1–6) is explicitly NOT public. A 2025 review states flatly there are no publicly available burn-depth segmentation datasets. (c) Verdict: training a "time until this box burns this skin" model from existing data is NOT feasible — the required (time, temperature, skin photo, outcome) tuples do not exist anywhere. The defensible design is exactly what's planned: physics-grounded conservative timers (CEM43/Moritz–Henriques thermal dose logic; moxa deliberately operates at 44–49°C where margins are tens of minutes, justifying the ~5-min check cadence) plus an escalate-only photo check tuned to obvious signs (blister, broken skin, marked erythema). Responsible new-data collection would be a prospective ethics-approved observational study in TCM clinics logging under-box skin temperature + timestamped calibrated photos + Fitzpatrick/Monk tone, with time-to-first-erythema (never induced burns) as the label — and even then burn events are too rare to label directly.

### Key facts

- **[established] No dataset exists pairing moxa/heat-device application time with skin outcome (time-to-erythema or time-to-burn) — public or restricted**  
  Searched academic literature, ClinicalTrials.gov moxibustion IRT trials (NCT05665426, NCT05578027), Zenodo/Figshare, and Chinese-language moxibustion (艾灸) safety studies. Moxibustion thermography papers publish temperature curves as figures only; the Solovchuk 2020 MRI-thermometry study states 'all data included in the paper' (i.e., no raw dataset), and it was ex vivo pork tissue anyway. This is an absence finding: state it plainly in the app design docs. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7071799/
- **[established] The only time-vs-skin-outcome data in existence is Moritz & Henriques 1947: burns do not occur below 44°C; at 44°C irreversible epidermal injury takes ~6 hours; damage rate rises logarithmically from 44°C to 70°C**  
  Am J Pathol 23:695-720, human and pig skin, full text free on PMC (PMC1934304). It is tabulated data from a 1947 paper, not a modern dataset: healthy young-adult skin, tiny n, sustained uniform contact, no images, no elderly subjects. The 2017 Martin & Falder review (Burns, PMID 28536038) confirms 44°C basal-layer threshold and pain onset just above 43°C, and warns that time-temperature relations for deeper burns and for children are extrapolated with limited clinical validation. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1934304/
- **[reported] Thermal-dose literature uses CEM43 (cumulative equivalent minutes at 43°C) with ~600 minutes as the skin burn-injury threshold, validated against the Moritz-Henriques data**  
  This gives AcuGuide a principled, citable basis for timer math: convert measured/assumed under-box temperature to CEM43 and keep sessions far below threshold. Reported in the ScienceDirect thermal-injury literature summary; I did not fetch the primary CEM43 validation paper, so treat the exact 600-min figure as reported rather than independently verified. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/thermal-injury
- **[reported] Measured moxa-box skin temperatures sit right in the low-temperature-burn band: ~44-49°C at 3 cm moxa-to-skin distance; 'optimal' stimulation is cited as 46-48°C**  
  From the moxibustion-efficacy literature (J Tradit Chin Med Sci): skin under a moxibustion box averaged above 44°C at 4 cm spacing and 44-49°C at 3 cm; 46-48°C is described as the range minimizing discomfort and damage. A separate 36-subject study (PMID 23598824) measured moxa-burner contact surfaces at 56.9±0.9°C (rubber layer) and 65.8±1.2°C (cautery) during treatment, skin +11°C peak at 4 minutes, and concluded 3 cm is the safe distance. Implication: moxa deliberately operates where the Moritz-Henriques margin is tens of minutes, not hours — which quantitatively justifies the practitioner's ~5-minute skin-check cadence and the 10-15 min session cap. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23598824/
- **[established] Moxibustion adverse-event evidence is case reports only: 64 published AE cases total (24 articles, 6 countries), 43 of them burns (67%); incidence is unknown; no duration-vs-outcome data**  
  Xu 2014 systematic review (PMC4058265): burns ranged from mild (34 of 37 newborn spark-moxibustion cases) to third-degree with eschar and disfiguring scars. Vulnerable groups explicitly documented: newborns, a 78-year-old diabetic, and a sensory-impaired patient — supporting the app's mandatory (non-skippable) checks for older adults. Park 2010 (PMID 21056845) likewise concludes incidence cannot be established for lack of prospective studies. No underlying dataset in either review. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058265/
- **[established] Low-temperature burn case literature confirms the elderly risk the practitioner described, with concrete numbers: ~44°C exposure for ~2 hours produced deep dermal burns in mobility-impaired elderly women**  
  Japanese bathing-scald case series (Cureus/PMC8701377): a 68-year-old and a 71-year-old (knee osteoarthritis limiting escape) sustained deep dermal burns at ~44°C bath temperature over ~2 h. Low-temperature burns are defined as prolonged contact at ~44-51°C. UK regional burns unit retrospective on hot-water-bottle burns (PMC3885202) is the equivalent device case series. All are case series, not datasets. Mechanism for elderly susceptibility: thinner epidermis, reduced microcirculation, reduced thermal sensation/neuropathy, slower reaction (StatPearls NBK430773; Burns 2009 'Burn treatment in the elderly'). — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8701377/
- **[established] BIP_US (University of Seville + Virgen del Rocio Hospital) is the standard 'open' burn image dataset and it has only 94 images**  
  94 clinical photos of superficial-dermal, deep-dermal, and full-thickness burns (plus graft/non-graft labels), predominantly light-skinned Spanish patients. Distributed as a zip from a personal university page (http://personal.us.es/rboloix/Burns_BIP_US_database.zip); described as open-access in papers but with no formal license I could verify. The 99%+ accuracies published on it come from augmenting 94 images to ~6,000 — a textbook overfitting setup; do not take those numbers as evidence a burn classifier is easy. — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-39618-0
- **[reported] Kaggle/Roboflow burn sets are ~1,300 web-scraped images with 1st/2nd/3rd-degree labels and unusable provenance for a shipped product**  
  The Kaggle 'Skin Burn Dataset' (shubhambaid) is ~1,300 web-scraped images labeled by burn degree; license not stated on accessible mirrors (Kaggle page blocks fetch; the E3S conference paper using it describes it as web-scraped). Roboflow hosts derivative object-detection versions (e.g. 'skin burn wound classification' by Atiya, claimed CC BY 4.0; 'Skin Burns' by AiBuildersClub) but a CC label on scraped medical photos does not confer real rights — no patient consent, no clinical ground truth, unknown duplication. Not appropriate for a medical-adjacent iOS app. — https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shubhambaid/skin-burn-dataset
- **[established] The only skin-tone-diverse clinical burn dataset (1,105 burn + 536 background images, Fitzpatrick 1-6, Sweden + South Africa) is explicitly NOT public**  
  Sci Rep 2023 (PMC9889389): 387 patients (51% children), acute burns photographed within 48 h, wounds cleaned and blisters removed before photography. Data availability: 'used under a license... available from the authors upon reasonable request.' Skin-tone performance is instructive for AcuGuide's escalate-only check: wound identification F1 was 76.9% on lighter (Fitzpatrick 1-2) vs 87.8% on darker (3-6) skin — burn wounds contrast MORE on dark skin, but early erythema contrasts LESS (see the 2025 Sci Rep cupping/skin-tone erythema imaging study), so an erythema-based check will have a skin-tone-dependent miss rate. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9889389/
- **[established] There are no publicly available burn-depth SEGMENTATION datasets at all, per 2025 literature**  
  Stated directly in the MDPI Sensors 2025 semi-supervised burn-segmentation paper; a 2026 scoping review of AI burn assessment (24 CNN studies, PMC12821601) finds datasets/labels/validation vary substantially and warns high reported performance does not imply clinical robustness. Also: every existing burn image dataset shows established wounds (often debrided, blisters removed) — none shows the early-warning states (first erythema, blanching response) a phone camera pointed at intact skin during a moxa session would need to recognize. — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/4/1059
- **[established] VERDICT: training a 'time until this box burns this skin' model from existing data is not feasible — the training tuples (time, temperature, skin appearance, outcome) do not exist anywhere**  
  Three independent gaps, each fatal alone: (1) no time-vs-outcome records beyond a 1947 table with no images and no elderly subjects; (2) no images of early heat erythema on intact skin — all burn image sets are established wounds; (3) no skin-tone-diverse public data. A regression model would also be unvalidatable: you cannot ethically generate test cases. Ship instead: conservative physics-grounded timers (CEM43 / 44°C-threshold logic; hard cap 10-15 min; mandatory ~5-min checks for older adults, consistent with the practitioner and with measured 44-49°C under-box temperatures) and a photo check that only detects OBVIOUS visible signs (blister, broken skin, strong erythema) and only ever escalates to stop — which matches the app's existing safety posture and never green-lights.
- **[established] Responsible new-data collection, if ever pursued: prospective observational logging in TCM clinics with time-to-first-erythema as the endpoint — never induced burns; burn events themselves are too rare to label**  
  Design that would actually work: ethics/IRB-approved observational study in moxibustion clinics (China/Korea/Japan for volume); log under-box skin temperature continuously (thin thermocouple or periodic IR spot reads), timestamped photos with a color-calibration card, Fitzpatrick/Monk tone, age, diabetes/neuropathy status; endpoint = first clinician-graded erythema or patient-reported heat pain (the UV minimal-erythema-dose paradigm is the ethical template — stop at first response). Use CEM43 thermal dose as the physical ground-truth covariate. Blunt caveat: with only 64 burn AE cases in the entire published literature, actual burn outcomes would be so sparse that the model would really be an erythema/thermal-dose model, not a burn model — which is another argument for shipping the physics timer and not promising burn prediction.

## 4. Computer vision for burn/skin assessment

Computer vision for burn/skin assessment is real but clinician-grade only where it uses special hardware or curated clinical data. (1) Burn-depth classification from RGB photos: research CNNs (ResNet50/101, VGG16, transfer learning) report 68.9-95.4% depth-classification accuracy vs a 66-75% baseline for experienced burn specialists, but on small single-center datasets with almost no external validation; the only FDA-authorized burn AI (Spectral AI DeepView, De Novo May 2026) is a cart-based 8-wavelength multispectral device for burn centers, NOT a phone photo product. No FDA-cleared RGB-photo-only burn assessment exists, clinical or consumer. (2) Erythema quantification from phone photos is confounded by unknown RGB color spaces, auto white balance and lighting; every credible system solves it with a physical color-calibration target in frame (Scarletred Vision, a CE Class Im medical device, uses a calibration sticker ON the skin; ColorChecker-based systems reach deltaE 1.8-6.6). Absolute redness from an uncalibrated consumer photo is not a defensible measurement. (3) Skin-tone equity is the hard gate: erythema in Fitzpatrick IV-VI presents violaceous/gray/brown or is invisible; dermatology AI trained on light-skin data drops 29-40% ROC-AUC on diverse images (DDI: e.g. 0.72 to 0.57). A redness heuristic will systematically under-detect early burns on darker skin — so a photo check must never be a clearance mechanism. Notably, a burn model trained on representative dark-skin data (Sweden/South Africa, Sci Rep 2023) had HIGHER sensitivity on darker skin (89.3% vs 78.6%), showing the gap is data, not physics — but AcuGuide has no such dataset. (4) On-device is trivially feasible (MobileNet-class models, ~89% on lesion tasks, <2s inference, few-MB CoreML) — compute is not the bottleneck; validated data is. (5) Consumer framing: apps that assess skin from photos either became regulated medical devices (SkinVision CE MDR Class IIa 'risk assessment', Google DermAssist CE Class I 'not intended to provide a diagnosis') or stay informational (Aysa: 'does not diagnose', suggests possibilities + see-a-provider). Under FDA's Jan 2026 General Wellness guidance, an app claiming to 'detect a burn' or 'assess skin damage' makes a device claim; a timed reminder to self-inspect plus educational stop guidance stays wellness-side. Nothing exists for moxibustion or low-temperature burn photo assessment — this is a green field with no validated model to borrow. RESPONSIBLE DESIGN for AcuGuide: the photo step should be a structured self-inspection ritual (camera as mirror/magnifier + prompts), not a CV verdict. If CV is added at all, limit it to (a) image-quality gating (is the site visible, in focus, well-lit) and (b) optional escalate-only change detection comparing against a baseline photo of the same site taken at session start on the same phone — each user as their own color control largely neutralizes white-balance and skin-tone offsets, and only a 'stop and cool the area / consider medical advice' output is ever shown; 'no finding' must render as 'no result — check by touch and feel', never as a green light. Never attempt burn-depth classification: low-temperature burns are characteristically deeper than they look at the surface, so a reassuring surface photo is exactly the failure mode. The mandatory ~5-minute checks must remain time- and symptom-driven (pain, persistent heat, numbness) for all users; the photo can escalate but can never satisfy the check on its own. This matches the planned 'photo can only ever escalate to stop' design — the research says that is the only defensible version.

## 5. Synthesis — what the app implements and why

### Session cap and default duration
Default session: 10-15 minutes (user-selectable within that band). Hard cap: 30 minutes, non-extendable — the timer ends the session and cannot be restarted at the same site without a cooldown. Present these in the Sources screen as 'a conservative design choice consistent with published practice', never as a cited standard, because no guideline states a numeric maximum.

*Rationale:* Textbook stick-moxa guidance is 10-15 min per point; RCT box protocols cluster at 15-30 min with box protocols most often ~30 min; the only formal self-administration consensus (Delphi, cited by Cochrane CD003928) is 30 min once daily; a 2025 knee-OA meta-analysis found indirect moxibustion effective at <30 min/session. So 10-15 min is the conservative low end of actual practice and 30 min is the defensible ceiling. Physics agrees: measured box sessions held skin >44°C for only ~7-10 min (Xu 2012, PMID 22997790), and at the 46-48°C 'therapeutic sweet spot' the ASTM C1055 time-to-necrosis is 22-90 min — a 30-min cap keeps a well-positioned box inside margin, and the 10-15 min default keeps it far inside. Critically, NO published source states a maximum continuous heat exposure at one site; the cap is derived, and the app copy must say so.  
  Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22997790/; https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003928.pub4/full; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1744388125000441; https://journals.lww.com/jinm/fulltext/2023/05030/operating_procedures_of_moxibustion_technology.11.aspx; https://www.astm.org/Standards/C1055.htm

### Skin-check cadence
Keep the 5-minute repeating interval, but add an EARLIER first check at 2-3 minutes for everyone (mandatory for the higher-risk group, one-tap dismissable for others). Schedule: first check at ~2.5 min, then every 5 min until session end, plus a final check at removal and a delayed 'check again' reminder after the session. Never let completing a check extend or restart the timer. For the higher-risk group all checks are non-dismissable (timer pauses coaching until confirmed).

*Rationale:* No published skin-check interval exists anywhere — RCT protocols, nursing procedures, and WHO-derived guidance give only qualitative monitoring. So 5 minutes is practice wisdom, but it is physics-consistent: at 47-48°C skin temperature (measured under real boxes), time-to-necrosis is ~22-45 min, so 5-min checks interpose 4-9 looks before injury. The gap is the start of the session: at 3 cm spacing skin reaches 45.9-47.8°C within 1 minute and measured peaks exceed 49°C, where time-to-injury is ~5-11 min — crossable within a single 5-min interval. Hence the first check must land when the box reaches steady state (2-3 min). A post-session check matters because up to ~1/3 of thermal damage accrues after source removal (Stoll & Chianta). Copy must frame the cadence as 'a conservative interval consistent with measured box temperatures and burn time-temperature data', not a cited guideline.  
  Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22997790/; https://www.astm.org/Standards/C1055.htm; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28536038/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7195554/; https://www.saiseikai.or.jp/medical/column/low_temperature_burns/

### Higher-risk gating question
Gate on risk factors, not age alone. Ask a short checklist before the first moxa session (and re-ask periodically, mirroring the existing MoxaScreeningVisit per-visit pattern): (1) Are you 65 or older? (2) Do you have diabetes, or any numbness / reduced feeling in the area you'll warm? (3) Do you have thin, fragile, or slow-healing skin, or take medicines that thin the skin (e.g. long-term steroids)? (4) Would you have trouble quickly removing the box yourself? ANY yes → mandatory-check mode (non-dismissable checks, first check 2-3 min). Additionally, a hard stop — not just mandatory checks — for 'no feeling at all at the site': people who cannot feel heat should not self-administer moxa, per uniform home-use guidance.

*Rationale:* Age is a real proxy — the burn-surgery series mean age is 64.5, elderly skin is measurably thinner (epidermis −6.4%/decade, dermis −20% by 60), and warm-detection and heat-pain thresholds converge with age until they can be 'indistinguishable' in the lower limb — but the mechanism is sensory and structural, not chronological. The burn case literature names the actual risk factors: sensory impairment, diabetes (a 78-year-old diabetic appears in the AE review), very young/old age. Diabetic neuropathy abolishes the heat warning entirely, at any age, and lower extremities are both the most common moxa burn site (46/82 in the Chinese series) and the worst-affected by age-related sensory loss (non-abdominal sites 9.37× more likely to be deep burns). A 55-year-old with diabetic neuropathy is higher-risk than a healthy 70-year-old; the checklist catches both. This is stricter than published practice (trials in 60-80-year-olds did not dose-reduce), which is fine — say the app goes beyond, not that literature requires it.  
  Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058265/; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00238-020-01717-w; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568163710000450; https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/24/8/1468/21555/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27156790/

### Time-to-burn model feasibility
Do not build a predictive 'time until this box burns this skin' model — it is not feasible now, and the app should not imply one exists. The honest alternative, which the timer should implement: hard-code the published time-temperature curve as fixed conservative bounds. Concretely: treat the Moritz-Henriques/ASTM C1055 relationship (44°C ≈ 6 h to epidermal necrosis, time halving per +1°C up to ~51°C: 46°C ~90 min, 47°C ~45 min, 48°C ~22 min, 49°C ~11 min, 50°C ~5-6 min) plus the measured under-box range (44-49°C at 3 cm spacing) as the design envelope, and derive the 10-15 min default, 30-min cap, and 2.5/5-min check schedule from it. Optionally cite the more conservative Japanese low-temperature-burn rules (46°C = 30-60 min, 50°C = 2-3 min) as the margin basis. Present as: 'Timers are set conservatively from published burn research; the app cannot measure your skin temperature or predict burns.'

*Rationale:* The training tuples (time, temperature, skin photo, outcome) do not exist anywhere — three fatal gaps: the only time-vs-outcome data is a 1947 table with no images and no elderly subjects; all public burn image datasets show established clinical wounds (BIP_US is 94 images; the only skin-tone-diverse set is not public; a 2025 review states no public burn-depth segmentation datasets exist), never the early erythema a phone would see; and a model would be unvalidatable since test cases cannot ethically be generated. Only 64 moxibustion adverse events have ever been published, with application duration 'not systematically documented' — burns are too rare and too undocumented to label. Fixed physics bounds, by contrast, rest on 75+ years of replicated burn science and require no per-user inference. One labeled extrapolation is reasonable: since the only age-adjusted guidance anywhere is pediatric (lower protection temperatures 3-4°C), the app may justify its stricter higher-risk schedule as an analogous, clearly-labeled conservative offset — an extrapolation, not a standard.  
  Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1934304/; https://www.astm.org/Standards/C1055.htm; https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/4/1059; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058265/; https://www.saiseikai.or.jp/medical/column/low_temperature_burns/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28536038/

### Strap vs tape fixation
Recommend a strapped-on box over taping, framed honestly as practitioner guidance supported by burn physics — not by comparative studies, because none exist. Copy pattern: 'Use a strap you can release in seconds. Don't tape the box down — tape slows removal and can press the hot surface against your skin.' Add three non-negotiable caveats to the setup flow: never lie on top of or against the box; never use it while lying in a position you can't quickly move from; never use it when drowsy or where you might fall asleep — the safety gate should ask 'Are you alert and able to remove the box quickly?' before the timer starts. Mention the padded-barrier idea (the one published detail: a trial wrapped the box in a velvet bag as a burn barrier) as an optional extra layer.

*Rationale:* No published strapped-vs-taped comparison was found — say so plainly in the Sources screen. But the physics case is strong: at ≥51°C skin temperature no check schedule can prevent injury (~3 min to necrosis), so instant removability is the only mitigation in the worst case; a box can measurably exceed 49°C at 3 cm and burning moxa is 548-890°C, so a slipped or compressed box escalates fast. Removal speed is exactly what tape degrades. The asleep/lying-down caveat is directly evidence-backed: home-use guidance uniformly warns against falling asleep during treatment; elderly bathing burns show ~44°C for ~2 h produces deep dermal burns when people cannot move away; hot-water-bottle contact burns cluster in the neurologically impaired; and the moxibustion burn series show these injuries are painless enough that victims present ~22 days late. The pain alarm cannot be trusted, so escape speed and posture are the controls.

## 6. Open questions

- China's national standard GB/T 21709.1-2008 (Technical Operational Specifications for Acupuncture and Moxibustion) is paywalled — its numeric durations, if any, were never directly verified. Worth buying/translating before claiming the Sources screen covers all standards.
- The 30-minute hard cap vs a stricter 15-minute cap for the higher-risk group: the evidence supports either; product needs to decide whether higher-risk users get a shorter cap (e.g. 15 min) or the same 30-min cap with mandatory checks. Physics (thinner skin, converged pain thresholds) argues for the shorter cap; no literature settles it.
- The CEM43 ~600-minute skin burn-injury threshold was seen only in a secondary summary, not verified against the primary CEM43 validation paper — verify before citing CEM43 arithmetic in user-facing sources.
- The 2025 knee-OA meta-analysis (<30 min/session finding) was read at abstract level only; full text is paywalled and should be checked before it anchors the cap citation.
- The under-box temperature evidence (Xu 2012) is n=3 at one acupoint with specific box geometry; real consumer boxes vary in moxa-to-skin distance and ventilation. Should the app ask which box type the user has, or state that timers assume a box maintaining ≥3-4 cm moxa-to-skin distance?
- If baseline-relative photo change detection ships, the escalation threshold (deltaE / a* shift) has no validation path — it would be a hand-tuned heuristic. Decide whether to ship tier 3 at all in v1, or start with tiers 1-2 (guided inspection + quality gating) only.
- Whether the app should ever pursue the responsible data path (IRB-approved observational clinic study logging under-box temperature, calibrated photos, and Fitzpatrick/Monk tone with time-to-first-erythema as the endpoint) — feasible in principle but a multi-year effort that would still yield an erythema model, not a burn model.
- How the mandatory-check mode interacts with accessibility: users with limited mobility may genuinely be unable to inspect a lower-back site every 5 minutes; the flow needs a caregiver-assisted or mirror-guided variant, which none of the research addresses.

## 7. Where the app deviates from the synthesis, and why

- **Cap 15 min, not 30.** The synthesis would defend a 30-minute cap from the trial corpus; the
  practitioner's band was 10-15 and the app keeps her top as the hard stop. Conservative by
  construction, one cap for everyone (a split cap invites picking the wrong one), and it also
  answers the open question about a shorter higher-risk cap: everyone gets the short one.
- **No baseline-relative color analysis (synthesis tier 3).** Shipped tiers are 1 only —
  guided inspection with a skin-tone-inclusive checklist. The deltaE threshold would be a
  hand-tuned heuristic with no validation path (open question above), and an escalation heuristic
  that under-reads dark skin is an equity failure even when it never green-lights. Revisit only
  with a validated, tone-diverse basis.
- **Age question asks 65+/thin-fragile-skin only.** The synthesis's fuller risk checklist
  (steroids, can-you-remove-it-yourself) is folded into the clock's setup copy (drowsy/lying
  rules) rather than more gate questions — the gate is five questions and each addition erodes
  answer quality. Diabetes/neuropathy and reduced feeling already block heat outright via the
  existing questions, which is stricter than the synthesis asks.
